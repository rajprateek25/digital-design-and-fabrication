# E-Textiles
In the exercise 4, the class was tasked to construct a custom wearable e-textile patch packing minimum **5 LEDs** for a passing grade and **8 LEDs** for a good grade, powered by a coin-cell battery.

---

## Gear & Material Used

* A sewable 3V CR2032 coin-cell battery holder stitched directly to the textile.
* Raw, uninsulated conductive thread sewn entirely by hand.
* 8 LEDs wired in parallel.

---

## Attempt 1

I have been a Batman fan since childhood. So for my first attempt, I wanted to design a Batman Logo. I carefully sketched the geometric logo shape first on paper and then used the paper as guide to cut the textiles.

The design looked great but I soon realised the flaws in my design. Placing all 8 LEDs in parallel paths into a tiny space was bound to result in **short circuits** since the conductive thread had no protective insulation and I am not that great at sewing things in place.

I realised that I need to revamp my design with more real-estate and lesser wedges so I have enough space to sew the positive and negative terminals from all the LEDs to avoid them from overlapping.

## Attempt 2

I ripped out the old stitches to reuse the thread and LEDs. I still wanted to create a batman logo so I searched online for inspiration and decided on a logo with straight lines, fewer wedges and more real-estate.

With the new design I also employed a two step strategy to place all LEDs and the battery back in place:

### 1. Spacing between LEDs

Before sewing them, I first placed all the LEDs at their intended places keeping the distance between them large to give the the wiring enough breathign room and while keeping the placement look aesthetically good.

### 2. Marking and color coding the conductive wire path 
before I picked up the needle to make a stitch, I grabbed two sketch pens to mark the wiring paths
* 🟦 **Blue Guidelines:** Marking the **positive ($+$) power rail** starting from the battery's positive terminal to the anodes of all 8 LEDs.
* 🟥 **Red Guidelines:** Marking the **negative (GND) return rail** starting from cathodes safely back to the battery's negative terminal.

This color coding worked as a schematic guiding my needle, allowing me to sew complex parallel paths without the challenge of overlapping wires which I encountered in my first attempt.

## Final Product

I succesfully replicated the batman logo design with 8 LEDs minimising the time spend at sewing, avoiding short circuitsa and loose connection knots at terminals.

Things that helped me succeed with minimum rework:
* **Parallel Connections vs Single Power Source:** In order for all 8 LEDs to have similar brightness intensity, it is essential to optimise the sewing path since the conductive thread acts as a high-resistance wire and all LEDs share single 3V coin-cell as power source.
* **Knot Securily:** The knots at the terminal became loose over time so I applied nailpaint remover to seal the knots in place to avoid loose connections and flickering.
* **Marking the Path:** Color coding the wiring path before begining to sew helped me plan for the possible wire overlaps and future short circuits, which in in the first attempt resulted in a lot of rework.
